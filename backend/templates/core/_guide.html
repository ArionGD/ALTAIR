What is ALTAIR?

ALTAIR scores public companies on financial "fragility" — how likely they are to be overvalued, over-leveraged, or unprotected if the market turns. Every number on this dashboard comes straight from the company's own financials and market data — there's no prediction of when anything will happen, only an assessment of how weak a given company looks right now. Higher scores = more fragile. This is a research tool, not investment advice.

Reading the Strike List table

Ticker
The stock's trading symbol. A .NS suffix means it trades on India's NSE (e.g. NYKAA.NS); no suffix usually means US-listed (e.g. AAPL).
Market / Sector
Which country the stock trades in (US or IND), and which industry group it belongs to (Auto, BFSI/banking, IT Service, Energy, etc.).
P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)
Roughly: how many years of current profit it would take to "earn back" the stock's price. A P/E of 500 means investors are paying 500× a year's profit — very expensive relative to a typical mature company (usually 15–25). High P/E without strong growth behind it is a classic sign of a "hyped" or overvalued stock.
Debt/Equity
How much the company owes in debt compared to what its shareholders actually own. High debt/equity means the company leans heavily on borrowed money — risky because interest has to be paid whether or not profits hold up.
Rev Growth (Revenue Growth)
How much sales grew (or shrank) year over year. Negative growth combined with high debt or a high P/E is a red flag — the business is shrinking while still priced for growth.
Bailout %
A rough 0–100 estimate of how likely a government or central bank would step in to rescue this company if it got into serious trouble. Large banks, national energy companies, and defense contractors score high (too important to fail); small consumer-tech startups score near 0 (no safety net).
Strike Score
ALTAIR's overall fragility score, combining the metrics above into one number. This is the main number to look at first — higher means more fragile (more "air" in the valuation, less protection if things go wrong); lower means more resilient or protected.

Under the hood: the forensic metrics

These feed the scoring engine and may appear in a ticker's detail panel. Each is a well-known forensic-accounting technique, not something ALTAIR invented.

Altman Z-Score
A classic bankruptcy-risk formula. Below 1.8 = "Distress Zone" (high bankruptcy risk). Above 3.0 = safe. Between the two is a grey zone.
Beneish M-Score
Flags possible earnings manipulation. A score above -1.78 suggests the company's reported numbers might be too good to be true.
Piotroski F-Score
A 0–9 checklist of financial health signals (profitability, leverage, efficiency trends). 8–9 = very strong ("Iron Fortress"). 0–2 = weak.
Sloan Ratio
Checks whether reported profit is backed by real cash or is mostly accounting paper-profit. Above 0.10 is a warning sign that earnings quality is poor.
ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)
How much real profit a company generates for every dollar it invests. Below 10% often means the company is effectively burning value even while it looks like it's "growing."
Debt/EBITDA (EBITDA Leverage)
Years of core earnings it would take to pay off all debt. Above 4× is considered a major red flag by bondholders and lenders.
Short Float
What percentage of available shares other investors are actively betting against (shorting). High short interest means professional/institutional money expects the price to fall.

Using this dashboard

  1. Overview — the big picture: how many tickers are scanned, the average and highest strike scores, and the two charts showing the most fragile names and sectors.
  2. Strike List — the full sortable table. Click any row to open a detail panel for that ticker.
  3. Past Analysis — older, one-off analysis snapshots saved from previous manual runs. Useful for context, but not live data.
  4. The Market dropdown (top of the page) filters everything to US or India only.
  5. Run Full Audit re-scrapes every ticker live and recalculates all scores — it takes a few minutes since it fetches data one ticker at a time.
